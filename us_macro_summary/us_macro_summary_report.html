Definitions

Core Capex YoY% NSA

Core Capex (CGNOXAY% Index) is Census M3 nondefense capital goods new orders excluding aircraft, shown as YoY%. It is the cleaner business-equipment / private capex signal inside durable goods.

  • Includes — nondefense capital goods orders such as industrial / farm / construction / mining machinery, computers & peripherals, communications & instruments, electrical equipment, heavy-duty trucks, medical equipment, office furniture, and related capital goods.
  • Excludes — defense capital goods and aircraft (commercial & defense aircraft are stripped out because they are large and volatile).

Not the same as headline Durable Goods Orders YoY (DGNOYOY Index), which still includes aircraft and other volatile components.

Non-residential construction / GDP

The first construction chart shows total non-residential put-in-place (private + public, Census SAAR) and that spending as a share of nominal GDP.

  • GDP — Bloomberg GDP CUR$ Index is quarterly seasonally adjusted annual rate in USD billions, not a raw quarter sum.
  • 3-month MA — monthly non-res SAAR is averaged over the latest 3 months so the construction window matches a GDP quarter. At a quarter-end the ratio is (Q avg of monthly SAAR) / that quarter’s GDP SAAR.
  • Tickers: private CNSTPRNR, public CNSTPUNR.

Sub-category trend labels

Improving / Softening / Mixed / Stable on Leading, Labor, etc. count the latest-cell green vs red colors for each row in that sub-category, using the same 14M table color scale as the heat-map.

  • More green latest cells → Improving; more red → Softening.
  • Mixed / Stable when |green − red| ≤ 1 (both sides → Mixed; one-sided or all near-median → Stable).
  • Inflation sub-category remaps Improving→Softening and Softening→Firming (lower inflation prints as green).

ISM PMI vs. S&P Global PMI

Both publish diffusion-index PMIs (readings above 50 = expansion vs. prior month), but the surveys are not comparable level-for-level.

  • Panel size — ISM: ~600–700 executives (~400 manufacturers, 20 industries). S&P Global: 1,300+ firms (~800 mfg, ~400 services) with SME/large stratification.
  • Weighting — ISM uses self-weighting panels; S&P Global GDP-weights sectors and firm size, US operations only.
  • Headline PMI — ISM: equal 20% on five components. S&P Global: heavier weight on forward-looking components.
  • Seasonal adj. — ISM pre-forecasts X-12 factors; S&P Global re-estimates monthly (X-13).

US GDP QoQ SAAR

Real GDP (GDP CQOQ Index) is the official BEA advance / second / third estimate of quarterly real GDP, percent change at a seasonally adjusted annual rate (QoQ SAAR). The table shows the print in the quarter-end month (Mar, Jun, Sep, Dec).

This is the published GDP growth rate. It is separate from Atlanta Fed GDPNow, which is a model nowcast of the current quarter in YoY%.

Atlanta Fed GDP Nowcast YoY%

GDPNow (GDGCAFJP Index) estimates current-quarter real GDP, updated daily. YoY% compares the nowcast-implied level to the same quarter one year ago.

  • Jul 2026 → Q3 2026 YoY% (vs Q3 2025)
  • From 1 Oct 2026 → Q4 2026 YoY% (vs Q4 2025)

Model nowcast only — not an official BEA GDP print.

Inflation Nowcast YoY%

Cleveland Fed inflation nowcast (CLEVCPYC Index) is a daily estimate of headline CPI year-over-year for the current period — the latest calendar month before the official BLS CPI release is final.

  • Jul 2026 column → nowcasted CPI YoY through Jul 2026 (vs Jul 2025)
  • Blends core/food/gasoline components using daily oil & weekly gasoline prices; revised each business day

Model nowcast only — not an official BLS CPI print.

PCE vs CPI

Both measure US consumer inflation, but they are not interchangeable. This dashboard shows BLS CPI YoY (CPI YOY / Core CPI XYOY; the Fed’s preferred target gauge is BEA PCE / Core PCE.

  • Scope — CPI is a fixed urban-consumer basket (BLS). PCE covers all personal consumption (BEA), including spending paid by third parties (e.g. employer-paid health insurance), so it is broader.
  • Weights — CPI uses relatively sticky survey-based expenditure shares. PCE uses continuously updated National Accounts shares (chain-weighted), so substitution toward cheaper goods is captured faster.
  • Housing — both include owners’ equivalent rent / shelter conceptually, but shelter has a larger weight in CPI than in PCE; sticky OER often keeps CPI hotter when rents lag.
  • Formula — CPI is a Laspeyres-style fixed basket; PCE is a Fisher chain index (geometric blend of Laspeyres/Paasche), which tends to run a bit lower over time.
  • Use — markets react first to CPI; FOMC communications and the 2% goal are framed in PCE / Core PCE. Core strips food & energy in both frameworks.

DXY Index

The ICE U.S. Dollar Index (DXY Curncy / DXY) measures the dollar against a fixed basket of six major currencies. A higher DXY means a stronger USD. This dashboard plots Bloomberg daily px_last for the latest 5 years.

Formula (geometric, weights sum to 100%):

DXY = 50.14348112 × EURUSD−0.576 × USDJPY0.136 × GBPUSD−0.119 × USDCAD0.091 × USDSEK0.042 × USDCHF0.036

  • Basket — EUR 57.6%, JPY 13.6%, GBP 11.9%, CAD 9.1%, SEK 4.2%, CHF 3.6%.
  • Quote convention — EURUSD and GBPUSD are USD per EUR/GBP (negative exponents: a weaker euro/pound raises DXY). JPY, CAD, SEK, CHF are foreign units per USD (positive exponents).
  • Constant — 50.14348112 rebases the index to 100 in March 1973.

Trade-weighted but not Fed TWI (that uses a broader, periodically updated country set). DXY is ICE-published, not a Fed or BLS series.

Vacancies-to-unemployed ratio

Job openings divided by the number of unemployed people. Both series are seasonally adjusted levels in thousands, so the ratio is openings per unemployed person.

  • Openings — BLS JOLTS total nonfarm job openings (JOLTTOTL Index).
  • Unemployed — BLS unemployed level (USUETOT Index).
  • A month is plotted only when both prints exist. JOLTS is released later, so the ratio stops at the latest openings month.

Labor force participation rate

BLS civilian labor force participation, seasonally adjusted, percent of the civilian noninstitutional population. The headline chart is total (PRUSTOT), men 16+ (PRUSM16S), and women 16+ (PRUSW16S).

The age charts use the same buckets for men and women: 16–19, 20–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, and 55 and over. Matching colors mean the same age group.

US CPI MoM contributions

Headline CPI CHNG Index is BLS CPI month-over-month, seasonally adjusted. The stacked bars are the MoM contribution of four Bloomberg baskets, in percentage points. They add up to headline MoM aside from a small residual.

  • Core Service — ECANXLPR Index.
  • Core Goods — ECAN4IJ3 Index.
  • Food — ECAN6AYN Index.
  • Energy — ECANZX50 Index.
  • Core Service baskets — shelter, medical care, transportation, education & communication, recreation, other personal services, water/sewer/trash, household operations.
  • Core Goods baskets — transportation less motor fuel, household furnishings, apparel, recreation, medical care, other goods, alcoholic beverages, education & communication.

GS Financial Condition Index (FCI)

Goldman Sachs US Financial Conditions Index (GSUSFCI Index) is a daily composite of US financial conditions — short and long-term interest rates, corporate credit spreads, equity valuations, and the trade-weighted US dollar.

  • Higher FCI = tighter conditions (headwind to growth)
  • Lower FCI = easier conditions (tailwind to growth)

Daily index; each month column shows the last observation in that calendar month. Not seasonally adjusted — no SA/NSA/SAAR label applies.

Citi Economic Surprise Index

Citi US Economic Surprise Index (CESIUSD Index) is a daily measure of whether recent US macro data releases are beating or missing Bloomberg/consensus forecasts.

  • Positive = data trending above expectations
  • Negative = data trending below expectations

Daily index; each month column shows the last observation in that calendar month.

Bloomberg ECO US Surprise Index

Bloomberg US Economic Surprise Index (ECSURPUS Index) tracks the cumulative surprise of US economic releases relative to economist forecasts.

  • Positive = releases surprising to the upside
  • Negative = releases surprising to the downside

Daily index; each month column shows the last observation in that calendar month.

GS MAP Economic Surprise Index

Goldman Sachs MAP US Macro Surprise Index (GSERMUS Index) measures how recent US macro data compare with GS MAP model expectations.

  • Elevated = data beating GS forecast framework
  • Depressed = data missing GS forecast framework

Daily index; each month column shows the last observation in that calendar month. MAP = Macro-data Assessment Platform.
