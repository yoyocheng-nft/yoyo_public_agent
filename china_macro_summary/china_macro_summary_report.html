Definitions

RatingDog PMI vs CFLP PMI

Both are diffusion PMIs (above 50 = expansion vs prior month), but they are not interchangeable level-for-level.

  • RatingDog (Caixin / S&P Global) — smaller-sample private-sector survey with heavier SME coverage; often more sensitive to external demand and private activity. Tickers: MPMICNMA, MPMICNSA, MPMICNCA.
  • CFLP / NBS official PMI — larger official panel weighted toward larger / state-linked firms; typically smoother and more domestically oriented. Tickers: CPMINDX, CPMINMAN, CPMICOMP.
  • Use — track both for breadth; divergences often flag private vs official / SME vs large-firm gaps rather than “one being wrong.”

Fixed Asset Investment (FAI)

NBS fixed-asset investment growth, shown as 3-month moving average YoY% (NSA). The headline FAI row is total investment; indented rows split the same concept into infrastructure, manufacturing, and real estate.

  • FAI 3MMA YoY% — total FAI (CNFQFA3M).
  • Infrastructure / Manufacturing / Real Estate — component 3MMA YoY% series under the same FAI release family.
  • Use — track the headline for the overall capex pulse, then the components for where stimulus or weakness is concentrated (property drag vs manufacturing / infra offset).

China Govt Bond 10Y Yield

The 10-year China government bond yield (GCNY10YR) is a benchmark long rate for sovereign funding and risk-free pricing.

  • Lower yield — usually signals easier financial conditions: stronger demand for bonds (safe-haven / duration), weaker growth or inflation expectations, and/or expectations of PBOC easing. It often accompanies softer activity data or policy support.
  • Higher yield — typically firmer growth/inflation expectations, less easing priced in, or reduced bond demand.
  • With USD/CNY — the chart under CPI pairs the 10Y with the dollar–yuan spot. A weaker CNY (higher USD/CNY) alongside falling yields can reflect growth caution and policy easing; the two series are complementary, not one-for-one.

Gold, crude & food wholesale

Six Bloomberg series plotted under CPI in two rows: gold / crude / pork, then beef / egg / vegetable. Each chart shows the level and calendar YoY% (print vs last observation on or before the same date a year earlier).

  • Gold spot — XAU Curncy, USD per troy ounce (London/spot gold).
  • Crude oil futures — CL1 COMB Comdty, front-month WTI combined generic, USD/bbl.
  • Pork wholesale — CNPORKWH Index, China wholesale pork price.
  • Beef wholesale — CNPOCATT Index, China wholesale beef/cattle price.
  • Egg wholesale — CNPOEGG Index, China wholesale egg price.
  • Vegetable wholesale — CNPOVEGE Index, China wholesale vegetable price.

Use 1Y for recent impulse, 5Y for cycle context. Food wholesale prices feed China CPI food; gold/oil are global risk and energy inputs.

LPR (Loan Prime Rate)

China’s Loan Prime Rate is the policy-linked lending benchmark banks use to price new loans (1Y and 5Y+ tenors). It is set monthly from quotes of designated banks, anchored to the PBOC’s medium-term lending facility (MLF) rate plus a bank spread.

  • Lower LPR — cheaper borrowing for households and firms (mortgages key off the 5Y LPR); a common easing signal.
  • Higher / unchanged LPR — less accommodation; banks or PBOC keeping lending rates steady.

RRR (Reserve Requirement Ratio)

The reserve requirement ratio is the share of deposits banks must hold at the PBOC. It is a structural liquidity tool, not a daily market rate.

  • RRR cut — frees bank reserves, adds system liquidity, and is typically read as easing / support for credit.
  • RRR hike — drains liquidity; tightening (rare in recent years).

Other PBOC policy rates

Common China policy / money-market anchors (not all shown in the table):

  • 7-day reverse repo — short-term open-market operations rate; the near-term policy signal for interbank liquidity.
  • MLF (Medium-term Lending Facility) — medium-term PBOC lending to banks; the key anchor under the LPR.
  • SLF (Standing Lending Facility) — ceiling-style facility for emergency bank liquidity; less used as a day-to-day signal.
  • Read-across — cuts in reverse repo / MLF / LPR or RRR generally ease conditions; rising 10Y yields against cuts can mean markets doubt growth or expect more easing later.
